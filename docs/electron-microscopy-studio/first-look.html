Electron Microscopy Studio · Get started

First look

Applies to Electron Microscopy Studio 2.1 and later

From the welcome screen to a measured distance and a Fourier transform in a few minutes, with the demo data that comes with the app. No download needed.

Open a demo

  1. Open Electron Microscopy Studio. The welcome screen offers Open Image… and Try Demo Data.
  2. Click Try Demo Data and choose Lattice Image.

The image opens in the Imaging workspace. The Calibration section of the inspector on the right shows the pixel size and where it came from.

Measure a distance

  1. Choose the Distance tool in the Measure section of the inspector, or press ⌃⌘2.
  2. Drag across the feature you want to measure, for example over ten lattice fringes.

The fringes of the demo image are 0.235 nm apart, so ten of them measure 2.35 nm. The measurement appears in the Measure section, in the unit of the image and in pixels. The Angle tool (⌃⌘3) and the Profile tool (⌃⌘4) work the same way; Pan (⌃⌘1) moves the picture again.

Look at the Fourier transform

  1. Under Imaging in the sidebar, choose FFT.
  2. The window shows the image and its Fourier transform side by side. Drag on the image to draw a square region and transform only that part.
  3. In the Spots section of the inspector, click Find Spots. The spots are ringed and listed.

A lattice image gives sharp spots in its transform, one pair for each set of fringes. The further out a spot lies, the finer the spacing it stands for.

Save the picture

Choose File › Export Image… (⇧⌘E).

Try the other demos

Try Demo Data, also under Help › Open Demo Data, has one document for every part of the app. Each opens where it belongs:

Demo Opens in
Lattice Image Imaging › Image
SEM Image (generated) Imaging › Image
Au Diffraction Pattern Diffraction
Ni EBSD Patterns (kikuchipy) EBSD › Patterns
Duplex Steel Orientation Map (orix) EBSD › Maps
EDX Spectrum Image Spectroscopy › EDS
SEM-EDS Spectrum (BAM TM002) Spectroscopy › EDS
EELS Spectrum Image Spectroscopy › EELS

The lattice image, the diffraction pattern, the SEM image and the two spectrum images are generated. The nickel patterns, the orientation map and the SEM-EDS spectrum are public data; Example data names their sources.
