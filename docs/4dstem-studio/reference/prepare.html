Prepare

Applies to 4DSTEM Studio 5.4 and later

Prepare is where the dataset is calibrated. This page lists every control of its inspector under the name the app shows.

Calibration

The Calibration section lists five steps and the voltage. Each row shows its state (Not set, From file, Measured, Manual, From session or Not quantitative) and has the action that sets it.

Step What it unlocks How it is set
Origin & probe Descan-corrected DPC, Bragg vectors, strain and reconstruction Calibrate Origin, or Use Detector Centre as Origin for a centre by hand
Ellipse distortion Corrects detector distortion in Bragg, strain and orientation coordinates Fit Detector Ellipse
R–Q rotation Scan-frame DPC and iDPC, calibrated reconstruction geometry Measure R–Q Rotation
Q pixel scale Reciprocal units, physical orientation matching, quantitative phase From the file, typed, or Calibrate from Selected Material
R pixel scale Real-space axes, scale bars, quantitative reconstruction sampling From the file or typed. It cannot be measured from the data: enter it from the acquisition parameters.

Voltage is the accelerating voltage in kV. It is read from the file when the file carries it. Without it, mrad and Å⁻¹ cannot be converted.

Typing a value into a row replaces the one that was there; the row's state becomes Manual.

Under the rows the verdict reads "Quantitative — all 5 steps set", or "Quantitative in 3 of 5 steps" with what is still needed. Point at a row to read its detail and what it unlocks.

Clear Calibration returns every step to Not set without reloading the file. The voltage stays. Results computed against the old values are discarded or should be rerun; the confirmation says which.

Fit diagnostics & advanced correction

This section is closed at first. It holds the choices behind the origin and what the fits reported.

Control What it does
Origin source Where the origin comes from: a fit, the file, the session, or a centre set by hand
Origin fit The surface fitted through the per-position origins: Constant, Plane (the default, as in py4DSTEM) or Parabola
Origin method Centre of mass is the fast default. Friedel finds the beam through a beam stop by the pattern's own symmetry; it is slower and meant for data whose direct beam is hidden.
Calibrate Origin Finds the beam centre at every scan position, fits the surface, and measures the probe radius
Origin from Bragg Peaks Follows the direct beam among the detected Bragg disks instead of the centre of mass. For crystals whose strong disks pull the centre of mass off the beam. Needs Detect All Disks first.
Measure R–Q Rotation Measures the rotation between the scan and the detector
Fit Distortion from Spots Measures the detector's stretch on the matrix crystal's own spots, for a scan of one crystal that has no ring. Nothing changes until you click Apply Distortion.
Flip 180° The rotation measurement cannot tell θ from θ + 180°. If iDPC contrast is inverted, flip it here.
Typed θ, Transposed, Apply Rotation Enter the R–Q rotation by hand in py4DSTEM's convention: the angle in degrees (QR_rotation_degrees; py4DSTEM's QR_rotation holds the same angle in radians) and the transpose (QR_flip)

What the fits report:

Row How to read it
Probe radius The measured radius of the direct beam in detector pixels. Disk detection builds its kernel at this radius.
Fit residual The RMS distance, in pixels, between the measured origins and the fitted surface. Small is good; a value over 1 px on a spot pattern brings the hint to use Origin from Bragg Peaks.
Positions used The fit is robust: positions whose origin lies far from the surface are excluded and the surface is fitted again. "Nothing excluded" means there was no outlier tail, not that every position measured well.
Q shell check The Q scale from a crystal assumes the innermost detected peak is the innermost allowed reflection. With two shells visible the app checks this; with one it cannot, and says so.
R–Q rotation The measured or typed rotation, with ⊤ when the detector axes are transposed
Spot distortion The stretch of the long over the short axis, its direction, and how far the detected spots are from the fitted ones

Ellipse correction

Control What it does
Fit annulus inner, Fit annulus outer The radii, in detector pixels from the origin, between which the ring is fitted. Choose an annulus around a ring that is present in every pattern.
Fit Ellipse Fits the ring in the annulus. The annulus must contain a ring with broad angular coverage.
Fit Anyway Offered when the app refuses a fit because only a few of 36 sectors carry ring signal. Spots from a few grains fit an ellipse as well as a distorted detector does, so use it only if the annulus holds exactly one ring. The result is marked "Fit anyway".
Semi-axis a, Semi-axis b, θ, Apply Ellipse Enter an ellipse by hand in py4DSTEM's convention: a is the longer semi-axis, θ the tilt of the a axis from qx in degrees. Only b/a and θ change the correction.

The Correction row shows the ellipse in use. It is applied to calibrated Bragg maps, strain and orientation. Residual shows the fit's residual and how many of 36 sectors carried signal.

A centre by hand

Use Detector Centre as Origin sets the origin to the centre of the virtual detector: put the detector on the direct beam first. Origin x and Origin y take the beam centre's column and row in pixels. Restore Fitted Origin brings back the fitted origin that the manual centre set aside.

Method

The calibration follows py4DSTEM: Savitzky et al. (2021), py4DSTEM: A Software Package for Four-Dimensional Scanning Transmission Electron Microscopy Data Analysis, Microsc. Microanal. 27, 712. The app's results are checked against it; the numbers are in the app's parity notes.

If a step is flagged, see Troubleshooting.
